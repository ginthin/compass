1 · PICK A REFERENCE

The secret weapon · Aesthetic Intelligence

An infra company with taste is almost unheard of.
That's exactly why it's an advantage.

From Pauline Brown (ex-Chairman, LVMH North America): aesthetics is a business advantage, not decoration — "the arousal of the senses" — and it drives longevity. As LVMH's Arnault put it: maybe nobody uses today's iPhone in 20 years, but people will still drink Dom Pérignon. Aesthetics = longevity. It's not just for fashion — a steakhouse wins on lighting, music and even the uniforms. So can an onchain-infra company. In our category, where everyone looks and sounds identical, taste is a moat almost nobody is contesting.

TOOL 1 · ATTUNEMENT

Feel it

Notice what feels sharp vs generic. Screenshot every ad that makes you stop. Build the swipe file. Taste is trained, not born.

TOOL 2 · INTERPRETATION

Decode it

Ask why it works. Monzo = clarity as trust. Gandhi = compression as memory. Name the mechanism, then reuse it.

TOOL 3 · ARTICULATION

Define our codes

Compass's aesthetic codes: lavender, the single sharp line, the proof card, plain-language clarity, the compass mark. Own them consistently until they're recognizable.

TOOL 4 · CURATION

Cut ruthlessly

Taste is as much what you reject as what you make. One idea per asset. Kill the second-best line. Less, but sharper.

Why this matters for the fundraise and for Eli: aesthetic intelligence is what separates a company people respect from a company people remember and root for. It compounds — a recognizable, tasteful brand lowers the cost of every future sale and every future raise. Building it now, while we're small, is the cheapest it will ever be. This board is where we practice it.

Monzo

"MONEY NEVER FELT LIKE MONZO" · Uncommon Creative Studio · 2024
Their first big campaign in 5 years. A 60" film of before/after comparisons — a tarantula on a head becomes a head-massager; nails on a chalkboard become a harp — showing money-stress turning into calm. Backed by an FT takeover and comparative out-of-home stills.
Why it works: it sells a feeling, not features. Banking is scary; Monzo makes it feel light. Radical clarity + emotion = trust from a challenger with no right to it yet.
STEAL → sell the feeling under the product (relief from the backlog, calm about custody). Before/after is a format we can own: "8 months → 8 days."
FIND IT: search "Monzo Money Never Felt Like Monzo Uncommon" on YouTube / Campaign.
"Menos Face,

Librerías Gandhi

YELLOW/BLACK BILLBOARDS · Saatchi & Saatchi · since 2001
Yellow background, black letters, one witty line — nothing else. Real lines: "Este no es un anuncio espectacular, es bastante normalito." · "Si quieres ver más carteleras como esta, compra más libros." · "Menos Face y más Book." Went viral before social existed.
Why it works: extreme compression + self-aware humor. It respects your intelligence and rewards you with a smile. People screenshot and quote it for years — free distribution.
STEAL → the single-line billboard as a core format. Yellow is theirs; lavender is ours. "Your backlog says: pick one." lives here.
FIND IT: search "Librerías Gandhi espectaculares frases" / images.

Nubank

ANTI-BUREAUCRACY PLATFORM · Wieden+Kennedy SP · 100M+ customers
Chose purple by literally asking "what's the most anti-bank color?" Built the whole brand as an antidote to bureaucracy — "menos burocracia" — and grew mostly by word-of-mouth, celebrating customer impact over product specs.
Why it works: it named an enemy everyone already hated (bureaucracy) and owned a color to signal "we're the opposite of them." Identity before features. Proof that LATAM rewards clarity + a villain.
STEAL → name our enemy: the integration backlog / the 8-month build. Own lavender the way they own purple. Sell outcomes, never tech.
FIND IT: search "Nubank purple anti-bank Wieden Kennedy campaign."
"Payments infrastructure
for the internet."

Stripe

DOCS-AS-MARKETING · sells to CTO + CEO (our exact room)
Owned a category in seven words. Made documentation so good developers evangelize it for free ("7 lines of code"). Publishes essays (Stripe Press) that CTOs cite. Infra made desirable.
Why it works: for technical buyers, trust is built through usefulness, not ads. Great docs = the best sales rep. Clarity at the category level ("X for Y") makes you instantly placeable.
STEAL → a 7-word category line. Treat our docs, Studio, CLI & MCP as marketing. Blog depth that buyers use to decide, not to be impressed.
FIND IT: browse stripe.com + Stripe Press; note the docs tone.
"Descubre de lo que
tu dinero es capaz."

Bitso

"PELÍCULA DE ACCIONES" · ~$5M action-movie spot · Mexico
LatAm's biggest crypto platform took investing mainstream by not sounding like crypto — real-life use cases, normal Spanish. Their big 2025 push was a cinematic action spot for stocks + yield, closing on "discover what your money is capable of."
Why it works: it speaks the language of the person who needs it, about their life — not the technology. Entertainment + a clear "your money can do more" promise. This is the Compass Earn promise, one layer up.
STEAL → the "your money can do more" promise, aimed at fintech founders instead of consumers — a space nobody occupies. Spanish, use-case-first.
FIND IT: search "Bitso Película de Acciones" on YouTube.

The do / don't (taste guardrails for creative)

✅ DO

One idea per asset · sell the feeling/outcome · a number on screen · plain language a CEO gets · a line sharp enough to screenshot · lavender + one accent · silence-friendly captions.

✕ DON'T

Jargon stacks ("orchestration primitives") · rocket emojis · "thrilled to announce" · six products crammed in one frame · stocky "diverse team high-fiving" · uncanny fake-realistic avatars for trust content · hype without proof.
