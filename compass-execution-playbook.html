01 · The three motions — everything we publish is one of these

Daily heartbeat · Weekly franchises · Occasional campaigns.

The #1 confusion to kill: "what do we make and when?" Answer: everything falls into exactly three motions, each with its own cadence, owner and how-it's-made. Learn these three and the whole calendar makes sense.

MOTION 1 · DAILY

The Heartbeat

What: 1 X post + engagement + a few outbound DMs. Keeps us present and top-of-feed.
Cadence: every weekday.
How made: Claude drafts from the franchise menu; poster approves against the guardrail checklist; ships same day — no meeting.
Who: rotates, but Gin owns consistency.

MOTION 2 · WEEKLY

The Franchises

What: our 8 recurring "shows" (episodes) — blog/video/LinkedIn. This is the bulk of our content.
Cadence: pick 2–4 episodes per week.
How made: chosen Monday, Claude drafts, quick gate if needed, ships Thu–Fri.
Who: each franchise has an owner (see §03).

MOTION 3 · OCCASIONAL

The Campaigns

What: a full multi-channel push around one big moment (a launch, a flagship partnership, a market entry). Runs ~2–3 weeks.
Cadence: max 1 running at a time.
How made: the full engine — brief → all channels → activation → review.
Who: Gin quarterbacks; team executes lanes.

How they relate: the heartbeat never stops. Franchises run every week on top of it. A campaign occasionally rises above both for a big moment — and when it does, some franchise episodes and daily posts simply become part of the campaign that week. Three layers, one calendar.
02 · The operating week — two scenarios, pick one

Same machine, two tempos. We recommend the high-tempo one.

One publish day (Thursday) means a quiet feed most of the week and a bottleneck if approvals slip. For a team that wants engagement and volume, spreading posts across the week beats bunching them. So here are two real options with honest pros/cons — and a recommendation.

SCENARIO A · "CLEAN LANES" — one publish day
MON

Decide

  • /weekly-signals digest AI
  • 30-min Eli + Gin Gin
  • Pick franchises + campaign beat
TUE

Draft

  • Claude drafts all AI
  • Visuals, activation list, video queue
WED

Approve

  • Claims/partners cleared CTO
  • Positioning pass, UTMs, Buffer
THU

Publish

  • X → LinkedIn → blog
  • Videos live · reply
FRI

Activate

  • Email if asset ready
  • Named-account DMs team
  • Signal sweep Gin
PROS · clean separation, easy to learn, low daily overhead, safe for approvals.
CONS · feed is quiet Mon–Wed, everything rides on Thursday, one slipped approval kills the week, less engagement (algorithms reward frequency).

Best when: a campaign is NOT running and we want a calm, predictable baseline.

SCENARIO B · "HIGH-TEMPO" — two publish days, no dedicated approval day ★ RECOMMENDED NOW
MON

Decide + draft

  • Digest + 30-min Eli + Gin Gin
  • Claude drafts the week same day AI
TUE

Publish #1

  • Batch 1: X + a franchise + video
  • Approvals happen inline/async (Slack 👍), not a whole day CTO
WED

Engage

  • Reply to everything, DMs to named accounts team
  • Draft batch 2 AI
THU

Publish #2

  • Batch 2: LinkedIn + blog/video + partner kit
  • Everyone reshares & replies
FRI

Activate + capture

  • Email if asset ready · more DMs
  • Signal sweep → Monday Gin
PROS · 2× the posting, feed alive all week, more engagement & reach, no single point of failure, approvals never block (async), faster tempo for the fundraise window.
CONS · slightly more daily attention, approvals must be genuinely fast (the discipline: 👍 within 2h or it ships on the rules), needs the guardrails + claims library solid so async approval is safe.

Why we recommend it now: we're pre-raise and need visibility + engagement, and posting frequency is the single biggest lever on reach. Cutting the dedicated Wednesday approval day (approvals go async) is what frees the room for a second publish day — more output, not more meetings. It only works because §14's "approve the criteria" makes most content ship without a gate.

Total human time either way: ~30 min Monday + fast async approvals + the DMs + sessions. The difference isn't more work — it's when content lands. During a campaign, Scenario B is the default; the campaign beats slot into the two publish days.

When something big happens, this kicks in.

A campaign isn't "more posts." It's a coordinated 2–3 week push where every channel plays a different role toward one commercial goal. Here's the exact assembly line — what gets made, uploaded where, by whom, when.

06 · Build this FIRST — the signup lifecycle, in detail

The warmest leads we have are falling into a hole.
Here's the exact plan to fix it.

People sign up for our API free, get one email, then silence. This is the fastest money on the table and the first thing we build. The rule that keeps it good: quality of touchpoints over quantity. Fewer, sharper, better-timed emails — triggered by what they actually do, never a spray.

THE TOUCHPOINT BUDGET (never be spammy)

Hard cap: no more than ~4 emails in the first 3 weeks to any signup, and each must carry real value (a link that unblocks, an asset, a genuine question). The moment someone replies or activates, they exit the automated track and a human takes over. Silence from us is better than noise.

Every email must pass one test: "would this be useful even if they never buy?" If no, don't send it.

SEGMENT FIRST (4 types)

Explorer (solo dev — help, don't sell) · Builder (eng at a real company — remove friction) · Buyer (fintech with a launch — route to a human fast) · Ghost (never activated — one honest question). Claude classifies each on signup from domain + intent + first actions.

The build sequence (what we do, in order)

WEEK 1

See the hole

Get access to signup + activation data. Export everyone. Claude enriches + scores. Answer: how big is the hole, who's in it, where do they drop.

WEEK 1–2

Hit the warm ones by hand

Top 20–30 (company domain + intent) get a personal note this week. Manual — and it teaches the automation what "good" looks like.

WEEK 2–3

Draft the sequences

Write each triggered email in the Compass voice. /signup-lifecycle drafts, humans approve every send at first (1-click queue).

WEEK 4+

Automate the safe ones

Once we trust a sequence, let it run auto — keeping the Buyer & heavy-usage triggers human. Measure replies, activations, meetings.

What we need to confirm to build (this is the ask to CTO/product): where signups live, what activation events we can see (key created, first call, usage volume), and whether we send via Mailchimp or a lifecycle tool. Signup volume & tooling still unknown — this is the half-day scoping task that unblocks everything.

07 · The build order — a 5-week sprint to the raise, NOT a 2-month plan

We're late July. Decks go out early September. So we move fast.

Forget "week 8+." The fundraise sets the clock: the machine has to be visibly working before the decks go out, because "we validated a repeatable motion" IS part of the pitch. Everything below is parallelized and compressed into ~5 weeks. Ambitious on purpose — this is the fundraise timeline, and AI is what makes it possible with a tiny team.

WEEK OF JUL 28 · everything at once

Foundations + signup, day 1

Lock the sentence · claims library v1 · guardrails · Attio fields + growth lists · connect sources. In parallel: pull the signup export, score it, and hit the top 20–30 by hand this week. Don't wait for anything to be perfect.

WEEK OF AUG 4 · the engine + first campaign live

Skills + Earn pilot launches

Stand up /weekly-signals, /campaign-brief, /activate-accounts. Launch the Earn Tier-1 campaign for real (high-tempo week). First HeyGen videos + the first product film in production. The machine is now running, not planned.

WEEK OF AUG 11 · proof + growth

LATAM sprint + Arca push

15 discovery calls underway (they double as market research). Arca case-study approvals chased hard. Africa cut running via Funa. Second campaign beat. Roadmap Sessions start booking.

WEEK OF AUG 18 · evidence accumulates

Results start showing

Sessions → pilots scoped. Signup sequences automated (the safe ones). Case study published. The 7/30-day reviews produce the numbers the deck needs: engagement on target accounts, meetings, pipeline influenced.

WEEK OF AUG 25 · package the story

Fundraise-ready

/reuse assembles the marketing traction slide: "validated motion — here's the proof." Investor-update narrative written. The machine + early results become part of the deck.

EARLY SEPT · decks out

Raise

Decks go out on a story that's already true: a working commercial engine, real pipeline, a repeatable motion the round scales. Then we keep running it, louder, with the capital.

How a team this small moves this fast: AI does the production, so "build the engine" is really "build the skills + templates once" (days, not months). We run the manual version and the automated version at the same time — hit accounts by hand THIS week while the sequences get built. We don't wait for perfect; we ship the pilot in week 2 and improve live. Aggressive but real — the only hard dependencies are the signup data access (CTO) and Arca's approvals. Chase both day one.

The raise doesn't wait, so neither do we.
Machine live in 2 weeks. Proof in 4. Decks out in 6.
