The core · 90 seconds · 4 beats

Memorize these four. Everything else is extra.

Each beat is 2–3 sentences. Say them in order and you've explained the whole thing. The bold words are your anchors — if you blank, just hit the bold.

1

The why

~20 sec
"We have the best product in our category — and the market doesn't get it fast enough. Confusion is our most expensive competitor. We're fixing that with a machine that makes Compass impossible to misunderstand, and turns attention into pipeline. And the timing matters: the raise funds marketing and sales, so we prove the motion works before decks go out."
Cue: start with the pain, not the plan. Energy up.
2

How it runs

~25 sec
"The machine runs on two things — a clock and triggers. The clock is a weekly rhythm that never stops: Monday we decide, mid-week we publish, Friday we activate. And triggers: when something happens in your world — you ship a feature, land a partner, hear an objection — you drop one line in Slack, and the AI turns it into content. Nobody writes from a blank page."
Cue: this is the part that removes their fear of "extra work." Slow down here.
3

Your job is tiny

~20 sec
"AI does the heavy lifting — the drafts, the videos, the analysis. None of you becomes a marketer. Your job is small: flag events in Slack, send a few DMs to your accounts, reshare, and show up to a 30-minute Monday. I run the machine; the AI writes. That's it."
Cue: look at each person as you say "your job is small." Relief is the goal.
4

How we start

~20 sec
"This week we build the foundations quietly — Eli keeps posting so we don't go dark. Next week, August 4, we launch our first campaign together, loud. Then we've got a 5-week sprint to real pipeline and a fundraise story. I'll walk each of you through your exact role right now."
Cue: end on momentum → then go into the role cards, person by person.

No surprises · the questions they'll throw + your answer

Prevent every bump. Have these ready.

Read each person's likely pushback and your one-line answer. If you know these cold, nothing catches you off guard.

AIDAR
"Is this going to eat engineering time?"
Your answer: "No — about 30 minutes a week total. A quick claim check in Slack, and occasionally a 15-minute interview that becomes a video. AI does everything else. Engineering is not a content team."
ELI
"Do I keep doing my posts? What changes for me?"
Your answer: "Keep going — don't stop this week, that's important. The only change is the AI will hand you drafts so it's easier, and I'll ask you to sign off the big campaign angles. Your voice stays yours."
FUNA / PETROS
"Is this just LATAM? What about my region / my deals?"
Your answer: "Same machine, your region. The AI localizes the same campaign for Africa/Middle East and drafts your outreach. Every partnership you land becomes free content and reach. You're in from day one."
ANYONE
"I'm not a writer / I'm not creative — what if I mess it up?"
Your answer: "You never write anything. You drop one honest line in Slack — 'we shipped X' or 'a customer asked Y' — and the AI turns it into the post. You literally can't mess up a Slack message."
ANYONE
"What exactly do I do, day to day?"
Your answer: "Four things: flag events in Slack, reply to comments on our posts, send a few DMs to your accounts (I'll draft them), and show up to the 30-min Monday. That's the whole commitment."
ANYONE
"What if we forget to flag things / it falls apart?"
Your answer: "It can't go blank — the weekly clock runs no matter what, and every Friday I sweep Attio and Slack for signals so nothing's forgotten. The machine catches what people miss."
SKEPTIC
"Does AI-generated content actually work? Won't it look fake?"
Your answer: "A human — me — approves everything, and it all rests on our real proof and claims. Videos are template-based, not fake avatars, and we launch with clean no-face videos. Nothing embarrassing ships; the claims library makes sure of it."
SKEPTIC
"Who owns [Buffer / Mailchimp / approvals]? Feels like a lot of loose ends."
Your answer: "Fair — so every owner is named: I own the machine and publishing, Aidar owns claim checks, Eli owns positioning and case-study sign-off. And we barely use tools — no Buffer yet, Gmail for personal email. Fewer moving parts than it looks."

Delivery · small things that make it land

How to say it, not just what.

  • Open with energy and the pain — "confusion is costing us deals" — not with a plan or a doc.
  • Slow down on beat 2 (clock + triggers) — that's the part that removes their fear. Let it land.
  • Say "your job is small" while looking at each person. Get a nod before moving on.
  • Go person by person for roles — name them, name their one job, get a yes. (Use the Team Playbook role cards.)
  • End on the date: "August 4, we go loud together." Momentum, not "any questions?"
  • If you don't know an answer: "Great question — let me confirm and get back to you." Never wing a claim.
