The whole model · in 2 pieces

Everything runs on two engines. That's it.

🕐

Engine 1 · The Clock

A fixed weekly rhythm that happens every week no matter what. Monday you decide, mid-week you publish, Friday you activate + capture. It never stops, so the feed is never dead — even in a quiet week.

⚡

Engine 2 · The Triggers

When something happens in the business (a feature ships, a partner signs, an objection comes up, a signup goes cold) → it fires a specific piece of content or action → with a named owner → and Norte drafts it.

Why this removes the fear: content is never "invented from zero." It's either the clock (routine) or a trigger (reaction to real events). Both have owners. Both hand the hard part to Norte. The machine literally cannot go blank.

What starts it each week · the ignition

Two moments turn the key. That's the whole "how do we begin."

FRIDAY · THE SWEEP

Gin (20 min): Norte pulls the week's signals from Attio, Slack, calendar and the changelog into one list. You skim it. This is the fuel for Monday. This is what makes sure nothing is forgotten.

MONDAY · THE DECISION

Gin + Eli (30 min): look at the signals, pick the week's content (which franchises have an episode, plus any campaign), assign owners. Norte drafts from there. This is the one standing meeting.

That's the ignition: Friday you gather the fuel, Monday you light it. Everything else flows automatically from those two moments. If you only protect two things on the calendar, protect these.

How to explain the whole thing to the team · in 3 sentences

Say exactly this.

"1 · The machine runs on a weekly clock — same rhythm every week, so the feed is never dead.

2 · And on triggers — when something happens in your world (you ship a feature, land a partner, hear an objection), you drop one line in Slack, and Norte turns it into content.

3 · Your job is tiny: flag the events, send a few DMs, reshare, show up Monday. Norte does the writing; Gin runs the machine. Nobody becomes a marketer."

If they remember one thing: "flag it in Slack, Norte writes it." That's the whole contribution the machine needs from each person.
