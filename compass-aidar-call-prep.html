Your playbook for the Aidar call.

He knows little about the marketing plan yet — so you're taking him from zero to "I get it, I'm in, here's what I'll do." This is your talk track: what to say, in what order, what to ask him for, and how to answer the questions a CTO will fire back.

⏱ ~30 min
🎯 Understand + approve + own his part
🚀 We start executing next week
Before you dial · your 3 goals

Land these three, in order.

  • 1 · He gets it — Aidar leaves understanding what we're building and why it matters for the raise (not "posting more," but a repeatable commercial engine).
  • 2 · He approves the execution — he agrees the plan is real and doable, and that engineering's role is small and specific (not a content burden).
  • 3 · He owns his 3 asks — signup data, fast async claim checks, and 15-min interviews that become videos. Walk away with a yes on each.

Mindset: Aidar is technical — he'll respect precision and honesty, and he'll be allergic to fluff. Lead with the problem and the system, not the creative. Show him it's engineered, measurable, and low-effort for his team. Share the execution doc (compass-execution-for-engineering.html) on screen as you go.

The call · minute by minute

What to say, in order.

0–3 min · OPEN — frame it

Why this call

SAY"Aidar — next week we start executing the whole marketing + commercial engine, and we launch our first campaign the week after. Before that, I want you to really understand how it works and what I need from engineering — which is small and specific. By the end you'll know exactly who does what, when, and how. Cool?"
3–8 min · THE PROBLEM & THE STAKES

Why this matters (tie it to the raise)

SAY"The honest problem: Compass is more capable than competitors but harder to understand. Confusion is our most expensive competitor — we lose deals before the comparison even happens. And the fundraise use-of-funds is marketing + sales, so investors will fund a proven motion, not a promise. We have ~5 weeks to make the engine visibly work before decks go out in September. That's the stakes."

Anchor: this isn't "more posts." It's building the commercial machine the round will scale — and proving it works first.

8–13 min · THE PLAN IN 60 SECONDS

What we're building (keep it simple)

SAY"One position, said the same way everywhere: Compass lets fintechs launch yield, credit, trading and tokenized products through one integration, non-custodial, live in days — we're the layer underneath, they're the hero. Three arguments only. AI does the production — I decide and approve. First campaign: 'Put the Money to Work' — Earn for stablecoin apps in LATAM. And the fastest money on the table: the signup black hole — people sign up for our API free and we ghost them. We fix that first."
13–22 min · THE EXECUTION (the core — share the doc)

How it actually runs

WALK HIM THROUGH THE EXECUTION DOC"Here's how it runs so you can see it's engineered, not chaos:" — then show: the weekly rhythm (Mon decide 30 min → produce → async approve → 2 publish days → activate), the RACI (everyone has a lane, nothing orphaned), and the 5-week sprint (build wk1, launch wk2, growth wk3, results wk4, package wk5).
THEN — the part he cares about"Engineering's role is deliberately tiny and specific: ~15 min a week to green-light any new technical claim, and occasionally a 15-minute interview where you or an engineer explains something and AI turns it into a video — you never have to 'make content.' Plus flag product signals when something ships. That's it."
22–28 min · THE ASKS (get commitments)

What I need from you — 3 things

Go through the 3 asks below one by one and get a yes + a rough when on each. This is the whole point of the call.

28–30 min · CLOSE

Confirm & next step

SAY"So to confirm: you'll get me the signup data by [day], you're good being the fast claim-checker (~15 min/week async in Slack), and you'll do the odd 15-min interview for videos. I'll send you the execution doc and tag you when I need the first claim check. We start next week. 🚀"
The 3 asks · get a yes on each

What engineering owns (small, specific).

1
🔴 Signup data access

An export of API/Studio signups — who signed up, when, and any activation event we can see (key created, first call, usage). This unblocks the signup lifecycle (our #1 fast win) and tells us who to contact. Hard dependency #1.

EFFORT: one export now + confirm what events we can see · WHEN: this week
2
Fast async claim checks

When a post uses a NEW technical number or architecture claim, you 👍 it in Slack (usually <2 min). Everything else ships on the approved claims library — so this is rare. Precision protects us; you're the guardrail.

EFFORT: ~15 min/week, async, no meetings
3
15-min video interviews (occasional)

For technical/explainer videos, you or an engineer answers questions for 15 min ("what did we build, why is it hard, what breaks without it") — AI turns it into the video. You never write or edit anything. This fixes why our current videos don't land.

EFFORT: ~15 min, every 1–2 weeks, only when there's something worth it
+
Bonus: flag product signals

When something ships or a customer does something notable, drop it in the signals channel. That's the raw material the machine turns into content. Zero extra work — just a line in Slack.

EFFORT: a Slack message when it happens

The reframe if he worries about time: "Engineering is NOT a content team. AI does production. Your team's total ask is ~30 minutes a week — a claim check and maybe one interview. That's the whole footprint."

What a CTO will ask · have these ready

Questions Aidar will fire back — and your answers.

"Is this going to eat engineering time?"
No. ~30 min/week total: a fast claim check + occasionally a 15-min interview. AI does production; the team never writes or edits content. That's the entire footprint.
"How do we not make false claims / overpromise?"
That's exactly why the claims library exists — every approved number with exact wording, and a hard rule: anything not on the list gets [NEEDS APPROVAL] and comes to you before it ships. Nothing about compliance/KYB or unverified metrics goes out. You're the technical guardrail; the system is built around it.
"Isn't AI-generated content going to look like slop / bots?"
Fair. Two safeguards: (1) a human (me) approves everything; (2) videos are built from a template, not a droning avatar — cuts, captions, proof cards, product UI. And we launch with no-face videos (screen + captions); HeyGen avatars are only used if they pass a quality test. Video never blocks us.
"Why the signup thing first? Why does it matter to me?"
Because it's the warmest money we have — people already raised their hand for the API and we ghost them. Fixing it converts existing interest at near-zero cost, AND the data tells us where people drop (a product signal for you too — setup friction, activation gaps). It's marketing + product research in one.
"What do you actually need from me to start?"
Three things (the asks): the signup data export, being the fast claim-checker, and the odd 15-min interview. Give me the data this week and we're moving.
"How will we know it's working?"
North star = qualified opportunities created or advanced by marketing — tracked in Attio with source tags. Plus meetings booked, accounts reactivated, pipeline influenced. Not likes. By week 4 we have real numbers for the deck.
"Are you sure we can do all this in 5 weeks with a tiny team?"
Yes — because AI is the marketing hours we don't have, and we run the manual + automated versions at the same time. We don't wait for perfect; we launch the pilot in week 2 and improve live. The only hard dependencies are your signup data and Arca's case-study approvals — that's why I'm asking day one.
If you remember one framing

"We're building the commercial engine the raise will scale — AI does the work, I own it, and engineering's whole job is ~30 minutes a week: keep our claims honest, and talk for 15 minutes when there's something worth explaining."
