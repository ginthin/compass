THE WEEKLY RHYTHM · plan first, build after

Two phases, in order. Plan the words, then produce the visuals.

This is the single most important thing to understand. The plan is approved as cheap, editable text in a Slack canvas. Only once it is locked does Norte build the heavy stuff (billboards and the rendered HTML). So nothing expensive is ever made on a draft, and no one waits on an upload to see the plan.

PHASE 1 · plan the words

In the war room canvas

Monday, Norte fills a canvas in #norte-warroom with the whole week: every post, thread, email and blog, by day and by channel. Gin and Eli review it right there, move and cut, and lock it. It is live in Slack instantly, no upload, so Eli in London sees it the moment it runs. Anything with numbers or a technical claim goes to Aidar before it locks.

PHASE 2 · build the visuals

Only after it is locked

Once the week is locked, you say "go" and Norte builds the billboards and the rendered HTML for the approved pieces. That is the polished visual version. You publish it to the site (or Drive) whenever you want. Because it is already approved, not one asset is wasted.

FEEDBACK · how review and rework happen

The war room is where content gets reworked, not just read.

Reviewing is not "approve or reject". You leave corrections, and Norte redoes the piece right there, as many rounds as it takes, until it is right. Two kinds of feedback, two homes.

CONTENT FEEDBACK · the weekly loop

In the war room

You review a piece and drop your corrections on it. Norte redoes that piece with your notes, in the war room, and you go again until you are happy. Then it locks. This is the everyday loop.

NORTE FEEDBACK · occasional

In #norte-feedback

Only when Norte has a general habit to fix (a tone tic, or if it ever slips a long dash back in). That note gets folded into the skill so Norte never repeats it. Rare, not the weekly flow.

How to leave content feedback (the format)

Keep it on the piece so nothing gets lost. Two easy ways:

On short pieces (posts, threads): reply in the war room with a quick note tagged to the day and piece. Format: FIX [day / piece]: what to change. Example: FIX Tue BNB thread: too long, cut to 4 posts. To approve a piece: react ✅ or reply lock.
On long pieces (blogs, newsletters): open the weekly Doc in Drive and use inline comments (highlight the text, comment). Best place for anything long. Norte reads those comments and redoes the piece.
Then Norte reworks, on its own. Every 15 minutes (8am to 9pm) Norte reads the war room and the Doc, takes each note, redoes the piece with the skills, and updates the war room canvas. The canvas is always the freshest, official version, look for the 🟡 Revised tag in the week table. The Drive Doc can fall behind, so the canvas is the source of truth. As many rounds as it takes, until it is right.

The two approvals (the gate)

Nothing gets built or published on one ok. There are two gates, and each one needs both Gin and Eli.

GATE 1 · copy approval

Approve the words

Both react ✅ (or reply lock) on the copy in the war room. Only then does Norte move to Phase 2 and build the assets: billboards, the campaign HTML, the video.

GATE 2 · package approval

Approve the finished package

Once the full package is built (assets plus the posting calendar), both approve the finished package in the war room. Only after this second double approval does it move to #content-product as a Draft, ready for a human to publish.

To only review and comment, you need nothing connected, just Slack and the Drive Doc. Connecting Slack and Drive inside your own Claude is only needed if you want to trigger a redo yourself. And a human always publishes, Norte never posts on its own.
SWEEPS · so nothing piles up unseen

If weeks stack up, Norte says hello.

Life gets busy and sometimes a week does not go out. Norte runs backlog sweeps so nothing quietly rots. It looks across the queued weeks and posts a friendly rundown in the war room: "hello, here is what is still pending, here is what we have not posted yet." So you always know where you stand and can catch up, instead of discovering a backlog by accident.

The point: the machine keeps proposing, and it also keeps you honest about what has not shipped. A sweep is a heads-up, not a nag: here is the pile, here is the fastest way to clear it.
THE PART THAT WAS MISSING · how everything shares one calendar

The content mix: three layers, stacked.

Campaigns, franchises and daily posts do not fight for the calendar. They stack. This is the heartbeat.

HEARTBEAT

1 · The baseline drumbeat

Quick posts so we are never silent, even in a dead week. This is the clock. It always runs.

FRANCHISE

2 · Recurring series (part of the heartbeat)

Named shows on a fixed day, like "Objection of the Week" every Tuesday. Reliable rails, so the calendar is never blank.

CAMPAIGN

3 · Bursts on top

A big moment (a launch, a partner) takes the prime slots for a few days, with its own arc. It sits on top of the heartbeat, it does not replace it.

The rule Norte follows to build a week: lay the heartbeat first (franchise day + a proof post), then drop the running campaigns into the prime slots, then fill the gaps with daily. Priority: partner-dated campaign, then our own campaign, then franchise, then daily. Never two big things on the same day.

HOW TO TURN IT ON

Open Scheduled in Cowork, find norte-weekly-plan, click Run now. Norte drafts this week and drops it in the war room. That is the machine turning on. The first run also pre-approves the Slack and Drive permissions.

The rule stays: the tasks do the writing and planning on the clock, but nothing publishes on its own. A human always approves and posts.

HOW EVERYONE PARTICIPATES · the franchises

Recurring shows. Everyone is part of them.

A franchise is a named series on a fixed day. Each one has a usual host, but they are open to everyone and the topics are diverse, so everyone does a bit of everything. You drop the raw material, Norte writes the episode. Nobody has to write.

The point: the host column is just who usually starts it, not a fence. Funa can drop an objection, Bezkat can drop a market take, sales can front the build story if they saw it land. Topics are diverse and everyone contributes across all of them. You drop the raw signal (an objection, a ship, a win, a story, a thought) into #marketing-signals, and Norte turns it into that week's episode.
Franchises rotate, you publish your turn, and more the merrier. Different weeks, different people front a franchise: Bezkat or Aidar on the build, Funa or commercial on a region or partner, Eli on the founder take, Gin on the objection. Norte tells you when it is your turn and hands you the finished piece, and publishing it is yours too: personal and founder pieces go out from your own account, official ones Gin publishes. When the official account or a campaign is posting, that does not mean anyone goes quiet. Everyone has Norte and the plugin, so post in your own voice too, on brand. And support Compass and each other: reshare, comment on and amplify the team's posts. Your audience is our audience. More voices, more reach.
