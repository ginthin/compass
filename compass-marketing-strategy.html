02 · The 3 angles, read this once and everything else makes sense

A buyer only ever asks three questions.
So we only ever tell three stories.

Here's the logic, and it's worth 60 seconds because everything downstream hangs on it. When a fintech considers Compass, three doubts run through their head, in order: "is this worth the trouble?" → "can I actually believe you?" → "and where is this whole thing going?" Those three doubts are the only three things we ever need to answer. So we built three angles, one per doubt. The rule: one piece of content = one angle. Not because rules are fun, because a market remembers what it hears repeated, and confuses everything else. Three answers, said the same way for months, is how a company nobody understood becomes a company everybody can repeat.

🛡️ THE BIG SWING, the regulated-access layer (Eli's thesis, in progress)

The move: Compass doesn't just connect fintechs to products, it controls who can access each regulated product, under what rules (country, sanctions, accredited status, KYB), enforced by the infrastructure. Gov bonds and institutional RWAs can't be handed to any wallet; today every fintech + issuer rebuilds that wall from scratch. Make it reusable and we become the layer to launch AND operate regulated products onchain, the language banks, funds and custodians buy.

The honest constraint (Eli ↔ Corsa): a regulated entity can't fully outsource KYB, it keeps final responsibility. So we don't "become the regulator." We integrate a compliance engine (Corsa or similar), receive an attestation, bind the approved company to its wallet, apply each product's rules, block the unauthorized, keep the audit trail.
Proof it's real: Chainlink ACE, Polygon T-REX ($100B), and our own BitGo conversation (30 institutional clients) is exactly this buyer.

Marketing's job now: seed the narrative ("tokenizing was the easy part; compliant distribution is the moat") while product/BD scope it. In progress, do NOT publish as a shipped capability yet. Category air-cover, not a product claim.

07 · Video, the deep rework · this is where we break out

Our videos don't land yet. Here's why, and the whole system to fix it.

Let's be honest first: the videos we make today mostly don't perform. That's not bad luck, it's a diagnosable problem, and once we name it, the fix is a system. Everything here is AI-produced. But AI only helps if we feed it the right raw material and point it at the right job. Read this as the full video playbook: why it's broken, the fix, the type library, and how each one gets made.

THE DIAGNOSIS, 3 reasons our current videos flop
1 · The curse of knowledge

Our engineer knows the product too well. He's cycled on it for months, so he explains it as if it's obvious to everyone, skipping the "why should I care?" a buyer needs. Expert ≠ communicator. This is normal and fixable: he's the wrong person to write the video, but the perfect person to source it.

2 · Feature-first, not outcome-first

The videos explain what we integrated, not what the buyer can now do. "IXS vaults are live on BNB Chain, integrated with…", that's an internal changelog read aloud. Nobody outside the company feels a thing. No outcome, no reason to watch.

3 · Announcement, no tension

They open with the news, not with a hook. No human stake, no problem, no "wait, that's me." A video that opens "we're excited to announce" has already lost, the first 3 seconds decide everything, and ours spend them on us instead of the viewer.

The pattern: our videos are made by the person who knows the most and cares about the tech, so they sound like documentation. Great source material, wrong storyteller.

THE FIX IN ONE SENTENCE

The engineer brings the truth. AI brings the story. Never the same step.

We split the two jobs that were jammed together. Substance (what's true, how it works, why it's hard, from the engineer/CTO) is separated from storytelling (the hook, the outcome, the human stake, from AI + marketing taste). The engineer is interviewed, not handed a camera to narrate. That single split is what unlocks video for us.

"BUT WE'RE NOT EDITORS AND CAN'T SPEND OUR LIVES ON THIS", correct. Here's how the editing actually happens.

The cuts, captions, B-roll and proof cards are NOT manual editing. Nobody sits in Premiere for a day. Two things do the work:

① A template, built once, reused forever

We design ONE branded template per video type (intro card, caption style, proof-card style, lower-thirds, outro). After that, every video is "drop the script + clips into the template", 20 minutes, not a day. The look is consistent because it's literally the same template.

② AI editing tools do the tedious parts

Auto-captions, auto-cuts, and scene changes are one click in tools like HeyGen (avatar + scenes), CapCut or Descript (auto-caption, "edit video by editing text," remove filler). We don't cut every 3s by hand, the tool does it from the script.

The honest workflow: Claude writes the script → HeyGen/tool generates + auto-captions → drop into our template → 10, 20 min human polish → ship. The "editing skill" is really "pick a tool and build one template." Confirm the tool (HeyGen + CapCut/Descript) in the pilot; building the templates is a one-time ~1-day task.

REAL-RECORD GUIDELINES, raw is good, but keep it RARE and high-impact

Real (non-AI) video isn't only for Eli, but it can't be many, because filming eats time we don't have. The rule: real-record only when authenticity is the whole point, and only for moments worth a "pum." Raw beats polished here, a Zoom clip or a phone video is perfect; don't over-produce.

✅ Worth a real record

Eli's conviction moments · a customer saying the result in their own words · a clip grabbed from a conference talk we're already giving · a sharp 60-sec answer captured off a Zoom we're already on · a founder's raw reaction to a real moment.

How to get them cheaply

Record the Zoom/Meet you're already in and clip 30, 60s. Ask the conference to send the recording (or film it on a phone). Never schedule a "shoot day", grab it from something already happening.

❌ Don't real-record

Explainers, product walkthroughs, objection answers, anything repeatable or high-volume, those are AI. Filming those by hand is where a small team drowns.

Budget: aim for ~1, 2 real-record moments a month, chosen because they're unmissable. Everything else is AI. Scarcity is what makes the real ones land.

WHY OUR AI VIDEOS WON'T FEEL LIKE BOTS

A talking avatar reading a paragraph is a boring bot. Ours won't be, because the template does the work: the avatar is a narrator over a moving canvas, proof cards, captions, product UI, cuts driven by the script, never a head droning to camera. Same reason: one good template, reused.

12 · Brand guardrails, what Compass is NOT and won't tolerate

The lines we don't cross, and exactly why each one makes us sharper.

Some of these feel restrictive ("no hype words?!"). They're the opposite, they're what lets us stand out. Every rule below has a commercial reason: a sharp brand is defined as much by what it refuses to say as by what it says. If a post touches any of these, it doesn't ship, no debate. Put this in front of anyone who posts for Compass, including future hires and agencies.

No politics. WHY: we sell in México, Nigeria, Kazakhstan, the US, the EU. A partisan take in one market loses us three others. Neutrality is a growth strategy, not timidity.
No religion, no social wedge issues. WHY: zero upside, infinite downside. It converts nobody into a customer and can lose everybody.
No unverified claims or numbers. WHY: our entire Angle-2 is "Proof, Not Promises." One made-up number and the whole proof brand collapses. [NEEDS APPROVAL], never a guess.
No invented customer quotes or results. WHY: customers who trust us with their name become case studies; a misquote loses them forever. Written approval, always.
No financial advice or price predictions. WHY: we're infrastructure, not an advisor, and "this token will moon" is regulatory and reputational poison for an infra company selling to institutions.
No attacking named competitors. WHY: naming them promotes them and makes us look small. We beat alternatives with receipts. Confidence doesn't punch down.
No hype words ("revolutionary", "seamless", "best-in-class", "the future of finance", "thrilled to announce", 🚀). WHY, this is the big one: hype words are what EVERY crypto company says, so they make us invisible. Clarity is the contrast that makes us stand out. The banned words aren't too bold, they're too generic. Standing out means sounding like nobody else in the feed.
No fake urgency or engagement bait. WHY: CTOs and CEOs, our buyers, are repelled by "🚨 you won't believe". It signals "not serious infrastructure."
No overclaiming custody or security. WHY: precision IS the product for a non-custodial company. Blur it to sound simpler and we lose the exact people (compliance, CTOs) we need to win.
No memes that punch down / stereotypes. WHY: we sell across regions and cultures. Wit travels; cheap shots make enemies in markets we want to enter.
No confidential pipeline in public. WHY: naming an in-negotiation company (WisdomTree, the fund, BitGo's clients) can kill the deal. Relationship owner approves first.
No same-text-everywhere. WHY: it trains the audience to skip us ("saw it already") and wastes 5 channels doing 1 channel's job.

Living document, add to it the moment a gray area appears in a real post. It lives next to the claims library and inside the AI prompt, so drafts respect it automatically. The through-line: we stand out by being the clearest, most credible, least-hype voice in a category drowning in noise.

14 · Decisions, set the rules once, then let it run

We don't approve every post. We approve the criteria.

The bottleneck to kill: meetings. We decide the rules once, the sentence, the claims library, the guardrails, the scoring rubric, the templates, and after that, most content ships without a meeting. A human gate stays only where it must.

A CONCRETE EXAMPLE, three posts, three paths (this is what "approve the criteria" means)
✅ SHIPS IMMEDIATELY

Post: a "Backlog Files" X post, "Building Earn in-house: 8 months. Integrating it: days."
Why no approval: known franchise, approved claim, approved sentence, no new positioning. It passes the guardrail checklist.
Who: whoever's posting hits publish. Time to live: today.

⚠️ ONE QUICK GATE

Post: a LinkedIn post claiming "our RWA latency is under X ms."
Why a gate: that's a NEW technical number, not in the claims library.
Path: CTO thumbs-up in Slack (2 min) → number added to claims library → then it ships and is reusable forever. Time: same day.

🛑 REAL DECISION

Post: "Compass now does compliance/KYB for regulated products."
Why it stops: that's the regulated thesis, new positioning, not shipped, sensitive.
Path: Eli + Gin + CTO decide if/how we talk about it at all. Not a post decision, a strategy decision. Time: deliberate.

That's the whole system: the first post never needs a meeting because we already agreed the rules. The third one does because it changes what Compass claims to be. Most content is the first kind, so most content just ships.

✅ SHIPS WITH NO MEETING

Anything using the approved sentence + approved claims + a known franchise + no new positioning. Daily posts, franchise episodes, routine partnership beats, signup emails. Who checks: the person posting, against the guardrail checklist. Speed: same day.

⚠️ NEEDS ONE QUICK GATE
