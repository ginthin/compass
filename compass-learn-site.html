Lesson 1

What is Compass?

Building onchain finance usually means wiring up every protocol and chain yourself. Compass gives your app or agent one interface instead. You say what the user wants, Compass finds the product, handles the routing and contract complexity, and hands back a transaction ready to sign.

One interface. The user signs. Compass never holds the funds.
Nova 70sLesson 2
Lesson 2

How Compass works

Your app starts with an intent: what the user wants to do. Compass turns it into a route across supported products and chains, prepares the transaction (bundling steps and handling gas where configured), and returns it to the wallet. The user signs, and the funds move straight into the product.

Intent → discovery → prepare → sign → execute. Custody never leaves the user.
Nova 90sLesson 4
Lesson 3

What non-custodial means

Custody is about one thing: who can actually move the funds. With Compass the user controls the key, the app or agent starts an action, the user signs, and the funds go from the user straight into the product, never through Compass.

Non-custodial is about control, not a promise that nothing can go wrong. Onchain products still carry risk, and we always say where it lives.

Every word, in 30 seconds.

Track 1 · Money, networks and wallets

The building blocks. Where value lives and how it moves.
Blockchain30s
A shared network that records transactions and runs rules no one controls alone.
Like a ledger every computer agrees on.
Onchain30s
Something that lives and runs on a blockchain.
A savings position on the network, not in a database.
Token30s
A digital asset represented on a blockchain.
USDC is a token; so is a tokenized share.
Stablecoin30s
A token designed to track a reference like the dollar.
1 USDC aims to stay worth about 1 dollar.
Wallet30s
The tool a user controls assets with and signs from.
MetaMask, or an embedded wallet in your app.
Private key30s
The secret that authorizes control of a wallet.
Lose it, lose access. Compass never holds it.
Transaction30s
An instruction submitted to a blockchain.
"Deposit 1,000 USDC into Earn" is one.
Gas30s
The fee to process a transaction.
A few cents, and Compass can sponsor it.
🔑

Track 2 · Control, custody and accounts

The most important track. Who can actually move the funds.
Custody30s
Who can actually move the funds.
A bank has custody of your cash; here the user keeps it.
Custodial30s
A provider controls the keys or assets for the user.
An exchange that holds your coins. Not us.
Non-custodial30s
The user keeps signing authority and control.
The user signs; no one else can move funds.
Self-custody30s
The user directly manages the wallet and recovery.
You hold your own keys and recovery phrase.
Product account30s
A programmable onchain account for product interactions.
The account an Earn position lives in.
Wallet connection30s
Permission for an app to read an address and request signatures.
Not a transfer of ownership.
Approval30s
Permission for a contract to use a token under set conditions.
Allow a contract to use up to 1,000 USDC.
Relay30s
A server submits a signed transaction for processing.
Relaying is not controlling funds.
⚙️

Track 3 · Protocols, contracts and execution

Under the hood. How the action actually happens.
Smart contract30s
Code on a blockchain that runs defined rules.
The Aave code that pays yield, automatically.
Protocol30s
A system of smart contracts providing a financial function.
Aave, Morpho and Pendle are protocols.
DeFi30s
Financial products delivered through blockchain protocols.
Lending or trading without a bank in the middle.
Intent30s
The result the user wants, not every technical step.
"Earn yield on my USDC" instead of ten steps.
Quote30s
An estimated result and terms for an action.
"About 4.8% APY on this deposit."
Routing30s
The path chosen to accomplish an action.
Compass picks Morpho over Aave for a better rate.
Payload30s
The structured data a wallet reviews and signs.
The exact data your wallet shows before you sign.
Finality30s
When a transaction is considered irreversible.
After confirmations, the deposit can't reverse.
Trust

Who controls the money.

Non-custodial, explained precisely

Every custody question has a clear answer. Compass never holds funds or keys.

Who controls the key?

The user.

Who initiates?

The app or agent.

Who signs?

The user.

Where do assets move?

User → protocol, never through Compass.

We never say non-custodial means safer or less risk. It describes the architecture, not a guarantee. Yield and trading always carry protocol, market and smart-contract risk, and we say so. ISO 27001. 99.99% uptime.
